# Marketing & Outbound: Emails, Landing Pages, Campaigns

Patterns for everything promotional or outbound: emails, landing pages,
paid/social copy, App Store text. All global rules from SKILL.md apply
(including the no-em-dash rule). Marketing is where the mission gets
slightly more room, but it's stated as a mechanic with receipts, never as
a sermon.

## The marketing posture

Angel marketing sells with receipts: names, numbers, dates, and the
member's role. The reader should finish knowing exactly what the thing is,
what it costs, and what their participation does.

- **Lead with the specific:** the shepherd, the premiere date, the price.
- **State the mechanic once:** "You fund the next season", "Tickets fund
  the next film", "Pay it forward. It's how the next family gets in."
  Then step aside.
- **Invitation, not demand.** Real deadlines are facts and fine to state
  (`Guild vote closes Sunday`). Manufactured urgency, fear-based framing,
  and negativity are banned. Angel sells hope with receipts.
- **Structure for skimming.** Lead with the audience benefit. Short
  paragraphs (1–3 sentences), bullets for lists, bold sparingly for key
  ideas, headlines under 10 words when possible. No long, dense blocks.

### The offerings, named correctly

When copy references an Angel product, use the canonical name and framing:

## Emails

Reads like a person wrote it, because the sender is Angel, a "we" with a
real voice, writing to one member.

**Subject lines:** sentence case, specific, no bait. The subject is a
promise the body must keep.

- `David S2 premieres Sunday at 8pm`, not "You won't BELIEVE what's coming 👀"
- `Your card was declined (2-minute fix)`. Transactional subjects state
  the problem and the size of the fix.
- `12 million gifts. Here's what they built`. Receipts as the hook.

**Body shape:**

1. One-sentence opener that gets to the point. No "We hope this email
   finds you well."
2. The specifics: what, when, how much, what the member's role is.
3. One CTA, whose verb matches the landing page.
4. Sign-off from a plain "The Angel team" (or a real name for personal
   sends). No inspirational-quote signatures.

**Transactional emails** (receipts, payment issues, account changes) follow
the error shape from ui-copy.md: reassure → explain → fix. They're the most
important emails we send. A member with a payment problem is deciding
whether we're trustworthy.

**Unsubscribe copy:** plain and respectful. `Unsubscribe`: one word, works
instantly, no "Are you sure you want to stop hearing about stories that
matter?" Guilt at the exit door undoes every trust deposit the brand made.

## Landing pages

- **Hero headline:** usually 6+ words → sentence case, no period. Lead with
  the story or the member's role: `Stories worth telling are worth
  protecting`, `You pick what gets made next`.
- **Section headers:** usually under 6 words → Title Case: `Featured
  Series`, `How Guild Works`.
- **Numbers as sections:** when you have a real number, give it room.
  `$20/mo` / `12M+ gifts` / `Sun 8pm`: big, plain, unadorned, each with a
  one-line caption naming what it is.
- **Proof over praise:** "From the creators of David" beats "critically
  acclaimed." Named people, real numbers, actual dates. Named proof must
  come from Angel's own titles, never "The Chosen" (a hard legal
  restriction; see SKILL.md).
- **One CTA per section**, verb matching the destination.

## Social & paid

- Same voice, shorter. The specific noun still has to be there. A post
  with no show name, no number, and no date shouldn't ship.
- Hype adjectives are still banned even where everyone else uses them.
  In a feed full of "EPIC 🔥 MUST-WATCH", plain specificity is the
  differentiator.
- Emoji: sparing, functional at most. Never stacked, never as a substitute
  for a noun.
- Don't punch at Hollywood. "We're not Hollywood" is posture we hold, not
  a taunt we post.

## App Store / store listings

- First line is the whole pitch and must stand alone: what you can watch
  and what it costs. `Watch free: David, Homestead, and more.`
- Feature bullets are facts: `Download episodes for offline`, `Cast to
  your TV`, `Join Guild to fund and vote on new shows`.
- No awards-speak, no "the #1 app for…" claims, no adjective pileups.
